# Storyboard design

## 1. Read the source material

Extract the script's narrative spine before inventing visuals:

1. Hook — the tension, question, or desired outcome.
2. Problem — what is difficult today and who experiences it.
3. Mechanism — how the product or idea works.
4. Evidence — UI, data, workflow, demonstration, or before/after proof.
5. Outcome — what changes for the audience.
6. Call to action — the next step.

Mark every factual claim as verified, supplied-but-unverified, or illustrative. Never fabricate product capabilities, customers, metrics, or endorsements.

## 2. Derive the visual system

Analyze the current project's references for:

- dominant and accent colors;
- typography weight, scale, density, and alignment;
- geometry, corner radius, borders, shadows, texture, and depth;
- camera language, pacing, transition grammar, and motion intensity;
- UI treatment, character treatment, photographic style, and brand restrictions.

Write a compact visual-direction statement before the table. The direction belongs to this project only. For example, a blue-white technology look may use cool gradients, fine grid lines, luminous data paths, and restrained glass layers; another project may require documentary warmth or editorial minimalism.

For a branded cover, specify its full first-frame layout, hold time, and how it enters the opening scene. Mark the corner overlays as persistent body layers and show where they clear for the end card.

## 3. Time the narration

Estimate spoken duration from the actual voice setting when available. Otherwise use the language-appropriate speaking rate and add pauses for comprehension. Do not force the script into a requested duration by accelerating speech beyond natural delivery.

Split shots around changes in meaning, evidence, location, interface state, speaker, or emotional beat. A shot may contain several coordinated layers; it is not synonymous with a slide.

## 4. Produce the storyboard table

Use this exact core schema. Add columns only when they change production decisions.

| 镜头序号 | 起止时间 | 对应口播脚本 | 叙事作用 | 画面设计 | 素材来源 | 动效设计 | 转场设计 | 声音设计 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Each row must be executable:

- `画面设计`: foreground, midground, background, framing, hierarchy, and readable on-screen text;
- `素材来源`: supplied file, screen recording, generated asset, stock, graphic build, or new capture;
- `动效设计`: what moves, from where, when, how far, easing, staging, and purpose;
- `转场设计`: the visual relationship between adjacent shots, not merely an effect name;
- `声音设计`: narration cue, music development, interface sound, impact, ambience, or deliberate silence.

## 5. Prevent the PPT look

Reject a storyboard that relies mainly on centered headings, repeated card layouts, or one screenshot per page. Improve it with:

- motivated camera movement through a continuous visual world;
- detail-to-context or context-to-detail reveals;
- UI focus pulls, masks, tracked callouts, cursor actions, and state changes;
- spatial data flows that connect cause, mechanism, and result;
- human moments that show who acts, decides, teaches, learns, or benefits;
- recurring visual motifs that evolve instead of resetting every shot;
- rhythm changes between dense explanation, proof, and breathing space.

Movement must communicate hierarchy or causality. Decorative motion that competes with narration should be removed.

## 6. Use people purposefully

Introduce a real, stock, or generated person only when the script refers to a user, role, decision, or lived outcome. Define role, age range, setting, action, expression, framing, and continuity. Avoid generic portraits that do not advance the story.

## 7. Gate 1 checklist

Before requesting approval, verify:

- every narration segment is mapped;
- total duration is plausible;
- product claims have a visible evidence strategy;
- text remains readable at delivery resolution;
- motion and transitions describe meaningful changes;
- characters and generated imagery are identified as such;
- the ending contains the required brand and call to action.

Present the table and ask for explicit storyboard approval. Do not begin asset generation or editing yet.
